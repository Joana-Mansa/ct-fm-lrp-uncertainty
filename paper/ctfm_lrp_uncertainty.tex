\title{Deletion-Based Faithfulness Metrics Do Not Discriminate\\
Between Attribution Methods on Volumetric CT}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Joana Owusu-Appiah}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Erasmus Mundus MSc Medical Imaging and Applications (MAIA)} \\
Girona, Spain \textbullet{} Dijon, France \textbullet{} Cassino, Italy \\
owusuappiahjoana59@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Attribution maps assert that the voxels they rank highest are those a model
responded to. Deletion metrics test this by masking top-ranked voxels and
recording the change in predicted probability. We apply the protocol to CT-FM,
a SegResNet pretrained by contrastive self-supervised learning on 148{,}000 CT
scans, fine-tuned for lung-nodule malignancy classification on volumetric chest
CT, and compare three layer-wise relevance propagation composites against
Grad-CAM. Two results are reported. First, CT-FM initialisation improves test
AUC over an architecturally identical randomly-initialised control at every
label budget, reaching 0.895 against 0.873 at full supervision; across three
seeds the mean gain is positive at each budget with overlapping ranges at 10\%
and 25\% of labels. Second, none of the four attribution methods achieved a
lower deletion AUC than a random-order baseline, scoring 0.12 to 0.22 lower on
AOPC. We attribute this to the masking scheme: random deletion distributes
replaced voxels uniformly through the volume and moves the input away from the
training distribution, whereas attribution-guided deletion removes a compact
contiguous region. Under mean-value masking the baseline therefore benefits from
distribution shift. Perturbation stability does separate the methods, with
Grad-CAM at 0.924 and the LRP composites between $-0.015$ and 0.220. A
four-member deep ensemble reduces expected calibration error from 0.127 to 0.051
relative to MC dropout and attributes 53\% of predictive entropy to model
disagreement against 2.4\% for dropout. We conclude that deletion protocols
require manifold-preserving masking before they can rank attribution methods on
volumetric medical data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two claims are common in the medical imaging literature. The first is that
self-supervised pretraining on large unlabelled image collections improves
downstream performance. The second is that a published attribution map describes
what the resulting model responded to. This paper evaluates both on a single
task.

We fine-tune CT-FM \cite{pai2025ctfm}, a SegResNet pretrained by contrastive
self-supervised learning on 148{,}000 CT scans, for benign-versus-malignant
lung-nodule classification on volumetric chest CT. We then explain it with
layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) \cite{bach2015lrp} implemented in zennit
\cite{anders2021zennit}, whose layer-type registry covers \texttt{Conv3d} and
the 3D pooling operators and therefore applies to volumetric networks without
modification. Grad-CAM \cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} is included as a baseline.

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A label-efficiency ablation against an architecturally identical
      from-scratch control at 10\%, 25\% and 100\% of labels, with a seed
      replicate that identifies which gains survive.
\item A faithfulness evaluation of three LRP composites and Grad-CAM on 3D CT
      against a random-order deletion baseline, with an analysis of the
      resulting negative outcome.
\item A measurement of explanation stability under input perturbation, and of
      the correlation between attribution faithfulness and predictive
      uncertainty.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Data}

NoduleMNIST3D \cite{yang2023medmnist} comprises 3D chest CT patches centred on
lung nodules drawn from LIDC-IDRI \cite{armato2011lidc} and labelled benign or
malignant. Splits are 1{,}158 training, 165 validation and 310 test volumes,
with a 863:295 benign-to-malignant training imbalance. We therefore use
class-weighted cross-entropy, report balanced accuracy and AUC alongside
accuracy, and select models on validation AUC.

We use the $64^3$ release. This is not cosmetic: CT-FM downsamples by a factor
of 16, so a $28^3$ volume is not divisible by the network stride and raises an
error.

\subsection{Model and implementation notes}

CT-FM's encoder produces a five-level feature pyramid; we take the bottleneck at
512 channels and $4^3$ resolution, apply global average pooling, LayerNorm and a
linear head. Three implementation details are recorded because each affects correctness
without producing an error message.

First, the published checkpoint does not load into MONAI's
\texttt{SegResNetDS}. Parameter names differ, and
\texttt{load\_state\_dict(strict=False)} matches \emph{zero of 161} tensors
without raising, so training proceeds on a randomly initialised network while
the code appears correct.

Second, the from-scratch control must be architecturally identical. Constructing
a second SegResNet from an inferred configuration yielded 19.9M parameters
against the true 87.2M; that ablation would have measured capacity and attributed
the difference to pretraining. Our control is the loaded model with every
parameter reinitialised.

Third, pooling the full SegResNet output gives the linear head two features,
since that output is a 2-channel volume. With unnormalised activations, whose
standard deviation is approximately 0.03, training loss remained at 6.37 with
AUC 0.50.

The encoder is fine-tuned at $10^{-5}$ and the head at $10^{-3}$. Fine-tuning
87M pretrained parameters at the head's learning rate displaces the pretrained
representation, which is the quantity under evaluation.

\subsection{Attribution}

LRP redistributes the output score backwards under conservation rules and
produces a map at input resolution. We compare three composites:
$\varepsilon$-plus-flat, $\varepsilon$-$\gamma$-box with box bounds matching the
$[-1,1]$ input range, and $\varepsilon$-$\alpha2\beta1$. All three are reported,
since the choice of rule affects the resulting map. Grad-CAM is computed on the
encoder bottleneck and trilinearly upsampled.

\subsection{Faithfulness, stability and uncertainty}

\textbf{Deletion} progressively replaces the highest-ranked voxels with the
dataset mean and tracks the target-class probability; a faithful map produces a
steep early drop and hence a low area under the curve. \textbf{Insertion}
restores highest-ranked voxels onto a blurred volume. \textbf{AOPC} is the mean
probability drop across deletion steps. Every map is accompanied by a
random-order baseline, because absolute values depend on model and data and only
the gap against random is interpretable.

\textbf{Stability} is the mean correlation between the map on a clean input and
on a noisy copy ($\sigma=0.05$), noise the model itself ignores.

\textbf{Uncertainty} is estimated by MC dropout over 20 passes, giving
predictive entropy and mutual information, plus expected calibration error.

CT-FM initialisation wins at every budget (Table~\ref{tab:ablation},
Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}), reaching 0.895 AUC and 0.830 balanced accuracy at full
supervision against 0.873 and 0.788 from scratch.

Across three seeds the mean gain is $+0.0198$ at 10\% and $+0.0263$ at 25\% of
labels, with overlapping ranges between the two arms at both budgets. One seed
at 10\% gave a higher score for the randomly-initialised arm. Table
\ref{tab:ablation} should be read as a single draw, and additional replicates
are required before the gains can be quoted as established.

\subsection{Calibration}

Expected calibration error under MC dropout is 0.145 with mean predictive
entropy 0.085: the model is confident and less accurate than its confidence
implies, the usual direction for a small fine-tuned network on an imbalanced
task.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../docs/figures/faithfulness.png}}
\caption{Deletion AUC against the random-order baseline, and AOPC relative to
random. All four attribution methods fall on the wrong side of the baseline.}
\label{fig:faith}
\end{figure}

A faithful map should sit \emph{below} the random baseline on deletion AUC. All
four sit well above it (Table~\ref{tab:faith}, Fig.~\ref{fig:faith}): removing
the voxels these methods rank highest damages the prediction \emph{less} than
removing random voxels.

The likely cause is the masking scheme. Random deletion distributes replaced
voxels uniformly through the volume; at 5\% this produces a volume unlike any CT
scan, and the network's output falls because the input is far from the training
distribution. Attribution-guided deletion removes a compact contiguous region,
which remains closer to a plausible scan. Under this protocol the random
baseline benefits from distribution shift.

Two consequences follow. Deletion with mean-value masking does not discriminate
between attribution methods on this task, and a manifold-preserving scheme such
as inpainting the removed region is required before the protocol can be used for
ranking. Insertion also fails to separate the methods, with all four recovering
to 0.972--0.974.

\subsection{Perturbation stability}

Under perturbation the methods diverge. Grad-CAM maps are nearly invariant
(0.924) while the LRP composites give 0.012, $-0.015$ and 0.220. Grad-CAM is
computed at $4^3$ and upsampled, which constrains how much the map can change.
LRP resolves to input resolution and is correspondingly more sensitive to input
perturbation. Neither metric reported here establishes which behaviour is
preferable.

\subsection{Deep ensemble and MC dropout}

Each full-label run stores a tagged checkpoint, so the seed replicates used for
Section~III-A also serve as ensemble members. Four independently trained models
were available, with individual test AUC 0.8951, 0.9093, 0.9111 and 0.8805.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../docs/figures/ensemble.png}}
\caption{Calibration error, the share of predictive entropy attributable to
model disagreement, and the entropy distribution, for MC dropout against a
four-member deep ensemble.}
\label{fig:ens}

The ensemble reduces expected calibration error from 0.1265 to 0.0505 at
comparable AUC (Table~\ref{tab:ens}, Fig.~\ref{fig:ens}). The two estimators
differ in how they apportion the uncertainty. Under MC dropout, mutual
information accounts for 2.4\% of predictive entropy, assigning almost all
reported uncertainty to ambiguity in the data. Under the ensemble it accounts
for 53.0\%, indicating disagreement between independently trained models that
dropout sampling on a single parameter set does not expose.

\subsection{Uncertainty and attribution faithfulness}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../docs/figures/uncertainty_vs_faithfulness.png}}
\caption{Per-sample predictive entropy against AOPC. Correlations are weak and
the three LRP composites agree with one another and disagree with Grad-CAM.}
\label{fig:unc}
\end{figure}

Spearman correlations between predictive entropy and per-sample AOPC are $+0.190$,
$+0.174$ and $+0.221$ for the three LRP composites and $-0.124$ for Grad-CAM.
The three LRP composites agree with each other and give weakly positive
correlations; Grad-CAM gives a weakly negative one. With 64 volumes none of
these correlations is statistically significant. The same question is posed for
segmentation by Salahuddin et al.~\cite{salahuddin2024renal}.

\section{Limitations}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports a single seed, and across three seeds the
ranges overlap at 10\% and 25\% of labels. The explanation analysis covers 64
volumes, which is sufficient to show that the deletion metric does not
discriminate but not to resolve a weak correlation. The deletion baseline
replaces voxels with the dataset mean; a blurred or inpainted baseline would
remain nearer the data distribution and may change the conclusion of
Section~III-C. Inputs are $64^3$ patches, not whole chest CT, so a model
pretrained on complete scans may not be used as intended. The deep ensemble has
four members, which is at the low end for stable uncertainty estimates, and its
members share a pretrained initialisation, so their diversity arises from data
ordering, subsampling and dropout only.

\section{Conclusion}

Self-supervised CT pretraining improved downstream nodule classification at
every label budget, with overlapping seed ranges at the two lower budgets. The
standard deletion protocol ranked all four attribution methods below a
random-order baseline. We attribute this to distribution shift introduced by
uniform voxel masking. Faithfulness metrics developed for 2D natural images
require manifold-preserving masking before they can rank attribution methods on
volumetric medical data. Reporting the random control is necessary, since
without it the same measurements would support a ranking of the four methods.

\section*{Reproducibility}

Code, result files and all figures are available at
\url{https://github.com/Joana-Mansa/ct-fm-lrp-uncertainty}. The full ablation
runs in approximately 14 minutes on one A100. On Volta hardware, \texttt{torch}
must be pinned to 2.5.1+cu121; releases from 2.14 ship an architecture list
beginning at \texttt{sm\_75} and every CUDA call fails.
