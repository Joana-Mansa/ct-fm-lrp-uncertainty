{\LARGE\bfseries\color{navy} CT Foundation Models, Attribution\par and Uncertainty}\par
{\large A controlled study on 3D lung-nodule patches}\par
\textbf{Joana Owusu-Appiah}\par
Technical project report. This is not a peer-reviewed publication.

\section*{Research question and contribution}
Does CT pretraining improve classification when labels are limited, and how do attribution maps behave under perturbation and uncertainty checks? This project fine-tunes the existing CT-FM encoder, compares matched scratch controls, and implements attribution, calibration and uncertainty experiments. CT-FM pretraining was performed by Pai et al.; it is not a contribution of this project.

\textbf{Summary.} Available paired runs show small mean AUC gains from pretraining. The seed-0 full-data AUC is 0.8951 versus 0.8733 from scratch, but a patch-level bootstrap interval for the difference includes zero. Current attribution experiments do not establish faithful or clinically validated explanations.

\section*{Real input data and labels}
NoduleMNIST3D64 is a MedMNIST v2 benchmark derived from LIDC-IDRI. It groups malignancy ratings 1/2 and 4/5 into two classes and excludes rating 3. The short labels ``benign'' and ``malignant'' are \textbf{rating-derived, not biopsy-confirmed diagnoses}. Splits contain 1,158 training, 165 validation and 310 test patches. The test set has 246 lower-rating and 64 higher-rating patches.

\section*{Model and classification results}
The official CT-FM encoder is loaded through \texttt{lighter\_zoo}. Its deepest 512-channel, $4^3$ feature map is pooled and passed through LayerNorm, dropout 0.3 and a linear classifier. The complete classifier has \textbf{77,763,042 parameters}. The scratch control reinitialises the same architecture. Inputs are $64^3$ uint8 patches scaled to $[-1,1]$, a benchmark adaptation rather than the original CT-FM full-scan preprocessing.

Matched arms share stratified label subsets for each seed. Training uses class-weighted cross-entropy, AdamW, encoder/head learning rates $10^{-5}$/$10^{-3}$ and 25 epochs. Validation AUC selects checkpoints. Only seed/budget combinations with both arms contribute to paired averages.

A 2,000-replicate stratified bootstrap of the seed-0 AUC difference gives a 95\% percentile interval of $[-0.0184,0.0659]$. It resamples patches, not patients, and does not include training variability. The seed-2 scratch AUC exceeds the corresponding pretrained AUC. Three completed pairs are recorded at each budget, but the seed-1 scratch checkpoint is unavailable. The seed-1 pretrained record AUC is 0.911077 versus 0.911141 on re-evaluation; the cause of this small discrepancy is unresolved. No universal superiority claim is supported.

\section*{Attribution, uncertainty and interpretation}
Three Zennit composites and bottleneck Grad-CAM are evaluated on 64 patches. Deletion replaces ranked voxels with the \emph{current batch mean}; insertion restores them from a $5^3$ average-blurred input. Stability uses only the first 16 patches, three noise repeats and sigma 0.05.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
Method & Deletion AUC & AOPC & AOPC minus random & Stability\\\midrule
Random & 0.5870 & 0.3679 & 0 & n/a\\
EpsilonPlusFlat & 0.8193 & 0.1466 & -0.2213 & 0.0124\\
EpsilonGammaBox & 0.8224 & 0.1436 & -0.2242 & -0.0146\\
EpsilonAlpha2Beta1 & 0.8164 & 0.1494 & -0.2185 & 0.2201\\
Grad-CAM & 0.7121 & 0.2487 & -0.1192 & 0.9235\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Random deletion causes a larger confidence drop than all four attribution methods. Faithfulness is not validated; masking-induced distribution shift remains a hypothesis. The Zennit implementation has no architecture-specific canonizer or validated relevance conservation for the residual and normalisation operations. Finite-map checks on two cases do not resolve that limitation. General conclusions about LRP would therefore be premature. Grad-CAM's coarse $4^3$ origin also helps explain its smoothness; stability is not proof of faithfulness.

Head-only MC dropout uses 20 passes. The 64-case ECE is 0.1453. On all 310 test patches, saved MC-dropout results have AUC 0.8960 and ECE 0.1265; a four-checkpoint probability ensemble has AUC 0.9085 and ECE 0.0505. The ensemble AUC is below the best member's 0.9111. These are test-set observations, not externally validated calibration claims.

Entropy/AOPC Spearman correlations range from -0.1245 to 0.2214. All four unadjusted two-sided p-values exceed 0.05 (0.079 to 0.327). No clear association is established. Low mutual information from head dropout does not prove aleatoric uncertainty or clinical reliability.

\section*{Verification, limits and reproduction}
The audit checked data provenance, six full-test checkpoint predictions, ensemble metrics, saved-array statistics and two attribution cases. Paired summaries use completed GitHub records; differing earlier server files are archived. Lower-budget scores and full scratch seed 1 are record-based because those checkpoints were unavailable. Full training and the original stochastic analysis were not repeated.

The distributed arrays lack patient/site identifiers. Split independence and possible overlap with the CT-FM pretraining collection were not independently audited. There is no external cohort, whole-CT evaluation, clinical reader study or validated explanation ground truth. These are the next validation needs, not completed achievements.
